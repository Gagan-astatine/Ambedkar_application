# Story Review: The Making of Annihilation of Caste

## Story Details
**ID**: `STORY-AOC-01`
**Title**: The Making of Annihilation of Caste
**Subtitle**: How a cancelled speech became a foundational text
**Author**: Editorial Team
**Status**: `published`

## Beats

### Beat 1: Narrative
- **Type**: Editorial narrative
- **Text**: In 1936, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was invited by the Jat-Pat-Todak Mandal of Lahore, a Hindu reformist group, to deliver a presidential address. However, upon reading the advanced draft of his speech, the committee found it too radical and cancelled the conference. Dr. Ambedkar published it himself.

### Beat 2: Excerpt
- **Type**: From the archive
- **Intro text**: Ambedkar explains the overwhelming response to the publication in his preface to the second edition.
- **Source**: Document `AMB-EN-V01`, PDF Page 40, Chunk `AMB-EN-V01-C000047`
- **Excerpt Text (from DB)**: "The speech prepared by me for the Jat-Pat-Todak Mandal of Lahore has had an astonishingly warm reception from the Hindu public for whom it was primarily intended. The English edition of one thousand five hundred was exhausted within two months of its publication... To satisfy this demand it has become necessary to issue a Second Edition."

### Beat 3: Narrative
- **Type**: Editorial narrative
- **Text**: At the core of his critique was the idea that caste could not be reformed; it had to be annihilated. He argued that the Hindu caste system prevented true fraternity and equality.

### Beat 4: Excerpt
- **Type**: From the archive
- **Intro text**: He challenges the very foundation of the caste system and asks what an ideal society should look like.
- **Source**: Document `AMB-EN-V01`, PDF Page 72, Chunk `AMB-EN-V01-C000111`
- **Excerpt Text (from DB)**: "What is your ideal society if you do not want caste is a question that is bound to be asked of you. If you ask me, my ideal would be a society based on Liberty, Equality and Fraternity. And why not ? What objection can there be to Fraternity ?"

### Beat 5: Narrative
- **Type**: Editorial narrative
- **Text**: Ambedkar also pointed out how the caste system actively prevented the upliftment of marginalized groups like the aborigines, arguing that the need to preserve caste prevented true fellow-feeling.

### Beat 6: Excerpt
- **Type**: From the archive
- **Intro text**: Here, he explains how caste preserves isolation and prevents civilizing efforts.
- **Source**: Document `AMB-EN-V01`, PDF Page 68, Chunk `AMB-EN-V01-C000100`
- **Excerpt Text (from DB)**: "What is the cause of this shameful state of affairs ? Why has no attempt been made to civilize these aborigines and to lead them to take to a more honourable way of making a living ? The Hindus will probably seek to account for this savage state of the aborigines by attributing to them congenital stupidity. They will probably not admit that the aborigines have remained savages because they had made no effort to civilize them..."
